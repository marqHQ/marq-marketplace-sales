# Strategy and Escalation Rules

## Selection principle

Recommend the smallest intervention that removes or tests the primary blocker. No more than two actions. Each action needs an owner, evidence of completion, and a decision it enables. Do not recommend “follow up,” “check in,” another generic meeting, or more content without a causal link to the blocker.

## Continue the current plan

Use when momentum is Green, the next customer commitment is stage-appropriate, and no more leveraged gap exists. Tighten the next step rather than adding activity.

## Re-run discovery

Use when pain is generic, root cause is assumed, impact is not decision-useful, desired outcome is unclear, or new stakeholders expose a different problem. The purpose is to decide whether there is a material problem worth solving—not to restart the entire sales process by habit.

Completion evidence: customer confirms the problem, root cause, consequence, desired outcome, and why action matters now.

## Multithread

Use when the deal depends on one contact, a required role is missing, or the champion cannot mobilize consensus. Name the exact missing role and the purpose of access. Do not ask for “more stakeholders” generically.

Completion evidence: direct engagement with the missing role and clarity on their requirements/support.

## Executive involvement

Use only for a defined peer-level objective: validate strategic priority with an economic buyer, resolve an executive-owned risk, create reciprocal alignment for a material partnership, or support a champion's internal decision. Never use an executive as a status-chasing tactic or manufactured pressure.

Completion evidence: explicit executive decision, sponsorship, or resolution of the named risk.

## Technical or security session

Use when a material requirement is unvalidated, technical risk blocks solution confirmation, or security review needs scoping. Include the customer's technical/security owner and Marq's appropriate specialist. Do not use as a substitute for business qualification.

Completion evidence: documented requirements, pass/fail outcome, owners for gaps, and effect on decision.

## Business-case review

Use when value is acknowledged but not strong enough for budget/priority approval, impact is based on seller assumptions, or the economic buyer needs an investment case. Build from customer inputs; do not fabricate ROI.

Completion evidence: buyer validates inputs, value, cost of inaction, investment, and decision use.

## Mutual action plan

Use when both sides have a credible critical event, multiple dependent steps, and willingness to assign owners/dates. Do not use a MAP to simulate collaboration on an unqualified deal.

Completion evidence: customer accepts or edits steps, owners, dates, decision criteria, and dependencies.

## Pilot

Use only when a bounded test resolves a specific, material proof gap that cannot be answered more cheaply. Define hypothesis, users/data, duration, success criteria, buyer commitment, decision owner, and post-pilot decision date. Avoid pilots used to defer a decision or obtain free services.

Completion evidence: agreed charter and decision based on measured result.

## Commercial restructuring

Use when value and intent are confirmed but scope, budget timing, packaging, or implementation sequence is the actual blocker. Preserve value; do not default to discounting. Any price, term, or concession requires appropriate authority.

Completion evidence: customer confirms revised structure resolves the blocker and approval path remains viable.

## Push the close date

Use when remaining buyer steps cannot fit, the critical event changed, or the date is seller-created. Move to the earliest evidence-supported period or leave the replacement open until a named validation step. A pushed date without strategy correction is hygiene, not acceleration.

## Remove from forecast

Recommend Not forecasted when the deal lacks a customer-backed in-period critical event, valid next step, feasible buying path, active champion, or required late-stage process. This can coexist with continued pursuit. Upside is not a parking lot for hope.

## Move to nurture

Use when fit and credible future potential exist but timing, budget, competing priority, or organizational change prevents an active buying process. Require a customer-grounded re-entry trigger and date/condition; otherwise close lost is more honest.

Completion evidence: explicit trigger such as budget cycle, leadership decision, contract expiry, project milestone, or named future date.

## Disqualify

Use when evidence shows no material need, unacceptable fit/size, no authority path after reasonable validation, no intent to act, prohibited commercial/technical requirement, or explicit customer rejection. Also use when repeated non-response follows a direct close-out attempt and no credible re-entry trigger exists.

State the reason and evidence. Do not disqualify merely because the rep executed poorly if the customer opportunity remains real.

## Decision matrix

| Primary condition | Default strategic response |
|---|---|
| Green, stage-appropriate customer step | Continue plan |
| Pain/root cause/impact unproven | Re-run discovery; business-case review if value inputs exist |
| Single-threaded or missing authority | Multithread; executive involvement only for a named executive decision |
| Material technical/security unknown | Technical/security session |
| Complex credible path with shared event | Mutual action plan |
| Specific proof gap not otherwise resolvable | Pilot |
| Confirmed value/intent, structure blocks approval | Commercial restructuring with authority |
| Timing false but opportunity active | Push close date and remove/downgrade forecast |
| No active buying process but credible future trigger | Nurture |
| No qualification or credible re-entry trigger | Disqualify |

When two actions are allowed, pair a customer-progress action with the necessary forecast/CRM correction. Do not stack two seller-activity actions.
