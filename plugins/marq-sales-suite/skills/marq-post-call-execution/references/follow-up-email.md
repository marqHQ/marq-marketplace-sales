# Follow-Up Email Rules

## V1 default

Draft a customer-ready email of roughly 90–160 words, excluding the signature. This is a provisional operating default because the founding repository contains templates but no corpus of approved sent emails. Prefer one clear CTA. Use bullets only when they materially improve clarity, usually for two to four commitments or priorities.

## Grounding

- State only customer-confirmed facts, demonstrated capabilities, and explicit commitments as facts.
- Omit Codex inference and internal risk language.
- A seller interpretation may appear only as a clearly framed question or suggestion.
- Paraphrase a customer statement when the meaning and strength remain intact. Do not put a paraphrase in quotation marks. Use an exact quote only when the transcript wording is verified and unusually useful.
- Never invent ROI, savings, deadlines, customer references, attachments, pricing, implementation promises, security claims, integrations, or product behavior.
- Include pricing or commercial terms only when they were explicitly discussed and accurately captured; flag the draft for commercial approval when the wording could create a commitment.
- Include product claims only when grounded in the call demonstration or an approved current product source. If verification is unavailable, omit the claim or mark an internal placeholder that must be resolved before sending.

## Structure

Use a specific subject. Open with thanks, recap the one or two most important customer priorities, confirm commitments and next step, and close with a single low-friction CTA. Avoid a feature dump, fake enthusiasm, and generic phrases such as “circle back,” “touch base,” or “transform your business.”

If the next step was agreed, confirm what, attendees/owners, and date. If it was not agreed, propose one option and ask for confirmation. Do not convert a recommended date into an appointment.

## Voice

Use Justin's direct, warm, conversational style only when Justin Warner is the sender or the user explicitly asks for it. Keep paragraphs short, use plain language, and minimize exclamation points.

For every other rep, use a neutral Marq baseline: concise, specific, warm, low-jargon, and relationship-oriented. Do not claim to reproduce an individual rep's voice until two or three approved sent-email examples from that rep are available. Rep examples should cover at least discovery and demo/later-stage follow-up; use them to learn recurring choices, not to copy private customer details.

## Pre-send check

Before presenting the draft as sendable, verify that the names, company, dates, time zone, commitments, attachments, pricing, product claims, and CTA match the evidence. The workflow still stops before sending and asks for explicit approval.
