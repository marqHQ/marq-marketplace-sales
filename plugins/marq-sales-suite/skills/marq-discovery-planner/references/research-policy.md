# Pre-call research policy

## Source set

Use only sources the user supplied or is authorized to access. Relevant sources include:

- HubSpot account, contact, deal, activity, stage, qualification fields, notes, and associations.
- Prior Gong calls and transcripts.
- Email history and calendar context.
- Customer product-usage, support, adoption, renewal, and account-health data for expansion calls.
- Approved internal account research, enablement, product documentation, pricing, security, customer proof, and partner records.
- The company's official website, leadership pages, investor relations, annual reports, regulatory filings, and press releases.
- Current, reputable news.
- LinkedIn or Sales Navigator for role, tenure, and organizational signals.
- Job postings for stated responsibilities, tooling, and investment themes.

Access only what is necessary for the call. Respect connector permissions and do not expose private emails, transcript details, usage data, or CRM fields beyond the seller's legitimate planning context.

## HubSpot record resolution

The rep may identify the target by deal name or account name. Do not require them to open HubSpot, find the record, or paste its URL.

When HubSpot is available:

1. Search the appropriate object using the supplied name and reasonable variants.
2. For a deal match, verify it with associated company/contact, pipeline, stage, owner, close date, meeting participants, or recent activity.
3. For a company match, inspect associated deals and select only when the meeting and deal context support the choice.
4. Capture the canonical HubSpot record URL for the brief.
5. If multiple candidates remain plausible, present only the distinguishing fields needed for the rep to choose. Never ask them to retrieve the links themselves.
6. If no match exists after reasonable searches, state that clearly. Continue using other authorized sources when possible, or ask for a spelling, contact, owner, or other distinguishing detail.

Record lookup is read-only. Finding the planning target does not authorize creating, merging, or updating HubSpot records.

## Evidence labels

Use these labels consistently:

- **Buyer-stated:** directly said by a buyer in a call or email. Include date and speaker when useful.
- **Internal record:** HubSpot, usage, support, partner, or account data. Include system/date and note staleness.
- **Fact:** directly supported by an authoritative current source and not contradicted.
- **Public signal:** public information that may be relevant but does not establish buyer pain or intent.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable interpretation or possible relevance; phrase it so the buyer can disconfirm it.
- **Unknown:** decision-relevant information not yet established.
- **Conflict:** two sources disagree or materially differ.

Never promote a public signal or title-based persona assumption into a fact about pain, priority, budget, authority, urgency, current tools, or buying intent.

## Source precedence

Use relevance, directness, authority, and recency together; do not apply recency mechanically.

For buyer needs and priorities:

1. Recent direct buyer statement in context.
2. Recent direct statement from another involved stakeholder.
3. Current CRM/email/account record with clear provenance.
4. Public company source.
5. Reputable third-party source.
6. Seller or model inference.

For Marq capabilities, packaging, proof, and policies:

1. Current approved Marq product, pricing, security, legal, or enablement source.
2. Current Marq help/developer documentation.
3. Current Marq customer story for that customer's outcome only.
4. Repository reference material, checked against current sources before buyer-facing use.
5. Seller memory or model inference, which must be validated internally before use.

For contact roles and employment, prefer the person's or company's current official information over stale CRM or old transcripts, while preserving the discrepancy.

## Conflict handling

When sources conflict:

1. Preserve both claims and their source dates.
2. Identify which difference affects the call decision.
3. Prefer the more direct, authoritative, and current source only when that preference is justified.
4. Convert unresolved conflicts into one concise confirmation question.
5. Do not average numbers, merge titles, or silently select the convenient version.

Examples:

- CRM says 200 users; a recent buyer email says the first phase is 40. Treat 40 as the current buyer-stated phase and flag the 200-user scope as stale or future-state until confirmed.
- A repository guide lists an integration, but current product documentation does not. Mark capability as `Internal validation required`; do not promise it.
- Public news describes an acquisition, but the buyer has not linked it to content operations. Mark it as a public signal and ask whether it changes the relevant workflow.

## Missing evidence

- Build a low-confidence plan when enough context exists to make useful hypotheses.
- State what was searched and what remains unknown only when that helps the seller judge confidence.
- Prioritize questions that resolve the highest-impact unknowns.
- Ask the user for missing access or context only when the plan would otherwise target the wrong account, meeting, or decision.
- Never invent trigger events, personalization, customer quotes, metrics, current technology, pain, or stakeholder roles.

## Research-specific cautions

- **Company website:** reliable for what the company says about itself, not internal priority or execution quality.
- **LinkedIn/Sales Navigator:** reliable for visible role history when current, not buying authority or personal goals.
- **News:** use reputable sources and distinguish event date from publication date.
- **Job postings:** evidence of a role or stated responsibility, not proof that a project is funded or broken.
- **Annual reports/earnings:** evidence of corporate priorities and risks, not proof that the contact owns them or that Marq is relevant.
- **Gong/email:** direct and valuable, but time-bound; distinguish exploration from commitment.
- **Usage data:** behavioral signal, not customer interpretation.
- **Approved internal research:** retain its citations and date; do not recycle unsupported calculations.

## Research-to-question test

Before using a research item, complete this logic:

`Evidence -> possible relevance -> what remains unknown -> neutral question -> decision affected`

If the item cannot change a question, proof choice, risk, or target exit, omit it from the brief.
